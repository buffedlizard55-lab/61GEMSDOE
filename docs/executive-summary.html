Executive summary

The 60-second version

  1. What is being scored. The DOE GEMS Prize scores fault predictions with a distance-weighted Tversky index (alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel). Phase 1 is scored against faults withheld from the data you are given.
  2. What that means operationally. Copying existing_faults.tif cannot work, and the measured evidence says it actively hurts: the best-scoring raster in the group's registry contains zero pixels on the visible catalogue.
  3. What this repository does. It inverts the isostatic gravity band for basement relief, turns the relief's horizontal-gradient ridges into multi-pixel ribbons, removes everything within a tuned radius of the previous catalogue, and emits a binary all-finite GeoTIFF on the competition grid.
  4. What to do with it. Open the home page, click the download button, and submit that file with the supplied note. Full walkthrough: How to submit.

How to make a submission (short form)

  1. Download the GeoTIFF from the home page.
  2. On the competition submission page, choose that file, paste the note, submit.
  3. Confirm the receipt shows a score, and record it (an organizer receipt is the only thing this project treats as a confirmed number).
