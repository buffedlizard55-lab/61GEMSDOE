Five candidate hypotheses not yet implemented in this repository

Each names its layers (competition band numbers, read from the GeoTIFF band descriptions), the physical signature targeted, why it should find a fault the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue lacks, and how it differs from anything already in this repo. Ranked by expected DTI improvement per unit of implementation cost. Every one of them is validatable on data already hash-pinned here — no new external download is required, which matters because this sandbox's egress allow-list admits only github.com, api.github.com, codeload.github.com, pypi.org and files.pythonhosted.org.

1. Basement-throw ranking with a polarity test — cost: low — partially tested, negative

Layers: band 13 (isostatic gravity anomaly) inverted by Parker–Oldenburg; band 15 (depth to basement) for calibration.
Signature: rank the step ridge by the depth difference over a fixed 600 m baseline along the local gradient direction (the apparent throw), instead of by |∇D|, which grows without bound as the smoothing scale shrinks.
Why it should work: a range-front fault has hundreds of metres of basement offset; an intrabasement density contact has a small apparent throw. Throw is the scale-invariant quantity.
Tested here: Spearman(throw, |∇D|) on the ribbon mask = +0.9986. The two rankings are numerically equivalent on this grid, so throw cannot re-rank the emission and cannot be used to differentiate this lane from a sibling. Recorded as a negative result; no submission slot spent.

2. Cover-thickness × step-concordance gate — cost: low — rank 1

Layers: band 15 (depth to basement surface, the regional Saltus & Jachens product), band 12/19 (detrended elevation and its slope), band 13 inverted.
Signature: emit only where the basement step is concordant with a thick, flat sedimentary section — i.e. where band 15 exceeds its local median and band 19 falls below its local median, so the step is buried under alluvium rather than expressed as a range front.
Why it should catch an unmapped fault: the catalogue is built by eye from surficial expression; a step under 300 m of fill has none. This is the only candidate whose selection criterion is the absence of what the catalogue keys on.
How it differs: the parallel session's field is emitted over the whole footprint with no cover term; this repo's E2 sweep used cover gates on the broken instrument and never re-tested them geometrically.
Validation: segment recovery in basin interiors, candidate vs random lines — the statistic already implemented in scripts/e3_build_submission.py.

3. Gravity–conductivity step concordance — cost: medium — rank 2

Layers: band 13 inverted (basement relief) and band 17 (conductivity surface, log10 S/m, measured range 0.19–4.97).
Signature: require the basement-depth step and a conductivity step to coincide within 300 m and to run parallel, using the second-order orientation order parameter R = |Σw e2iθ|/Σw over both fields.
Why it should catch an unmapped fault: a fault that displaces basement also breaks the conductive cover sequence; two independent physics agreeing on a line and a strike is the criterion an expert mapper uses before adding a fault under cover. Neither field alone is in the catalogue's evidence base.
How it differs: nothing in this repository combines gravity-derived structure with the MT layer; band 17 currently enters only as a scalar.
Risk: this is the natural home of a different lane (magnetotelluric). Check the 3-px overlap and rank correlation against that lane's registry raster before emitting.

4. Step-polarity asymmetry: which side is down — cost: medium — rank 3

Layers: band 13 inverted, band 12 (detrended elevation), band 4/7/8 (geodetic strain).
Signature: classify each basement step as basin-side-down or range-side-down and keep only basin-side-down steps whose surface expression in band 12 is absent or reversed. Basin-and-Range range fronts are listric normal faults with a characteristic hanging-wall tilt; a density contact has no such asymmetry.
Why it should catch an unmapped fault: it is the direct test of this lane's named competing explanation (density contrasts inside basement), so it converts the run card's weakest point into a discriminator.
How it differs: the current emission is polarity-blind; it cannot tell a fault step from a pluton margin.

5. Along-strike persistence of the basement step — cost: medium — rank 4

Layers: band 13 inverted.
Signature: integrate the step amplitude along the ridge (a 15-pixel collinearity vote on the ridge skeleton) rather than pointwise. Faults are kilometre-scale linear objects; noise ridges are not.
Why it should catch an unmapped fault: an expert accepts a fault under cover when collinear segments persist across several kilometres; pointwise gradient maxima accept any local bump.
How it differs: every operator in this repository is local (3–5 px). Nothing aggregates evidence along a candidate line.
Note: the same idea appears in another lane's hypothesis list as “aligned drainage-valley chains”. If implemented, re-run the uniqueness check against that lane's raster before promoting.
