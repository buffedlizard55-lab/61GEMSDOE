Hypotheses

Ranking rule: expected DTI gain = (independent evidence x expected coverage of a catalogue-missing population) / implementation cost

#1 · H61-A · Basement-depth step ribbons from an independent Parker-Oldenburg inversion IMPLEMENTED AND VALIDATED THIS SESSION
Supplied layers used13 iso_grav_anom (input to the inversion)
18 iso_grav_anom_hg (fused as an edge strength)
own inversion -> depth-to-basement surface
own inversion -> horizontal-gradient maxima + measured throw
Physical signatureA basin-margin or intrabasin normal fault puts a step in the basement surface. Gravity of a thin slab is proportional to its thickness, so the step appears as a ridge of the horizontal-gradient magnitude of the depth-to-basement surface. The ridge's throw (depth difference across the ridge) is the fault's displacement.
Why it catches a fault the catalogue misses80% of Nevada is covered by Cenozoic deposits and these are thicker than 1 km over about 20% of the state (Blakely & Jachens 1991). A fault under kilometre-scale fill has no surface trace to map; the catalogue is built from surface mapping, so it is blind exactly there. The catalogue reports the fault only if a younger scarp or a mapped unit contact exposes it.
How it differs from everything already implementedThe sibling repositories invert nothing: they use band 18 (the supplied horizontal gradient) directly, or proximity/strike statistics of the catalogue. This lane re-derives the depth surface from band 13 with the non-linear Parker series, calibrates the density contrast against GP-1012 rather than trusting the supplied slab product, and then filters candidates by MEASURED THROW -- which is what makes a lineament a fault (DD-ABOUT).
Costone calibrable inversion (seconds at 400 m) + one EDT at 100 m

#2 · H61-B · Concealed-step index from the throw/depth ratio (buried scarp sharpness) NOT IMPLEMENTED (blocked, see names)
Supplied layers used13 iso_grav_anom
own inverted depth surface
15 depth_to_base_surf
Physical signatureThe ratio of throw to the depth of burial is a proxy for whether a step is a fault plane or a depositional ramp: a fault keeps a sharp step under fill, whereas a ramp is smoothed by the same fill.
Why it catches a fault the catalogue missesSame concealment argument as H-A, plus a filter that removes the drainage/topographic-artefact lineaments the older instruments emit.
How it differs from everything already implementedKeys on the ratio, not the magnitude -- no sibling instrument uses an inverted-surface throw/depth ratio.
Costlow (one extra ratio field) but needs a truth proxy to calibrate the sharpness cut
Blocked onA population of KNOWN buried faults with independently measured throws to calibrate the sharpness cut. Named free official source: USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database slip-rate/throw attributes (https://doi.org/10.5066/P9BCVRCK) joined to the INGENIOUS Great Basin compilation (https://doi.org/10.15121/1881483); obtainability checked, both are public downloads.

#3 · H61-C · Basement-fabric (trend) blocking: steps aligned with the magnetic fabric NOT IMPLEMENTED (blocked, see names)
Supplied layers used13 iso_grav_anom
14 tmi
2 rtp
9 tmi_vg
own depth surface
Physical signatureIn the Walker Lane the basement fabric sets the fault trend. A gravity step whose azimuth agrees with the local magnetic-fabric azimuth is more likely to be a basement-cutting fault than a shallow sedimentary edge.
Why it catches a fault the catalogue missesAdds a *consistency* test between two independent physics (density and magnetisation) that a surface-mapping catalogue cannot perform at all.
How it differs from everything already implementedUses the magnetic field as a fabric ORACLE for the gravity step, not as another edge detector.
Costmedium (structure-tensor azimuth of two gridded fields)
Blocked onA second magnetic grid at a different flight-line spacing to test azimuth stability. Named free official source: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic surveys, ScienceBase 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7 / doi:10.5066/P93LGLVQ (area 2 lines 400 m apart, azimuth 90 deg, CC0) -- obtainability checked: the ScienceBase release is public, but the grids are GB-scale and cannot be fetched from this sandbox.

#5 · H61-E · Along-strike relief partitioning between adjacent inverted basins NOT IMPLEMENTED (blocked, see names)
Supplied layers used13 iso_grav_anom
own inverted depth surface
12/19 detrended elevation
Physical signatureTwo adjacent basins whose depth maxima face each other across a narrow high are separated by an accommodation zone; a fault with opposite dip sense usually occupies it. The signature is a paired depth minimum flanking a narrow basement high at the same along-strike position.
Why it catches a fault the catalogue missesAccommodation zones are usually covered by the basin fill they separate -- the classic buried transfer zone.
How it differs from everything already implementedKeys on the JOINT geometry of two basins rather than on any single edge, so it cannot be reproduced by thresholding any single supplied band.
Costhigh (paired-object matching, no supplied layer does it)
Blocked onA buried accommodation zone with independent sub-surface control to test against. Named free official source: USGS/DOE geothermal well temperature logs and the GBCGE Subsurface Database (doi:10.15121/1987556); obtainability NOT yet verified here.
