#2 H61-I — Piedmont relay belt: basinward splays beyond the mapped range front

LayersH61-G inverted depth 0-contour and ≥300 m fill mask; band 12 detrended elevation/curvature and band 19 slope-ridge orientation; visible catalogue traces only for per-fold pruning.
Physical signature / transformA narrow, depth-conditioned belt 0–2 km basinward of a range-front step, with aligned but discontinuous short slope ridges marking young relay or antithetic splays.
Why it catches catalogue-missing faultsMain range-front faults are mapped more often than low-relief basinward splays; the target signature is a spatially distinct class rather than another copy of the main trace.
Difference from this repo / siblingsNo current repo candidate defines this offset belt from the inverted contact; H61-G's X2 uses an in-corridor slope ridge broadly, not a basinward relay geometry.
Expected DTI gainRank 2, medium qualitative potential; no score estimate. Compare against the exact 0.064939 shared-evaluator baseline with identical spatial folds and budget.
Cost / dataMedium. Existing layers are cached, but mask/feature construction must be repeated from visible faults per holdout fold; one pre-registered test only after an authorized run card.

#3 H61-H — Gravity step × magnetic edge, restricted to basin-interior contacts

LayersH61-G inverted gravity-step corridor; supplied total magnetic intensity band 3, tilt band 6 and vertical-gradient band 9; basin-depth mask.
Physical signature / transformA candidate fault that offsets magnetic basement or a magnetic flow may align a magnetic gradient/tilt edge with the gravity step; a nonmagnetic lithologic density contact may not.
Why it catches catalogue-missing faultsTests whether independent magnetic texture supports a fault interpretation where gravity alone has a named intrabasement-density-contact mimic. A missing magnetic edge does not rule out a nonmagnetic fault or contrast, so the test is asymmetric.
Difference from this repo / siblingsEarlier H61-C only proposed magnetic fabric as an oracle; H61-G did not use the magnetic stack. This would be a conjunction rather than a standalone magnetic detector.
Expected DTI gainRank 3, low-to-medium qualitative potential; intrabasement contrasts are not resolved by the current evidence, and no score projection is justified. Must beat the same-version 0.064939 baseline on identical folds.
Cost / dataMedium. Bands are cached; feature leakage and alignment must be checked per fold. Do not claim it removes density mimics without independent lithology/density evidence.

#4 H61-J — Gravity source-depth consistency filter for basement versus intrabasement contacts

LayersIsostatic gravity band 13, horizontal/vertical derivatives bands 5 and 11, H61-G inverted fill-depth surface; only derive catalogue-conditioned features from visible faults.
Physical signature / transformEstimate contact/source depth from gravity derivatives; retain step maxima whose source depth is compatible with local basin-fill thickness, and flag deeper sources as possible intrabasement contrasts.
Why it catches catalogue-missing faultsDirectly interrogates the lane's named competing mechanism rather than assuming every basement-depth step is a fault.
Difference from this repo / siblingsNot implemented in this repo or measured in the H61-G experiment; it is a new falsification test of X1-type structures, not another slope-ridge locator.
Expected DTI gainRank 4, uncertain/low until source-depth calibration is validated; no score projection. Any retained candidate must beat the same-version 0.064939 baseline on identical folds.
Cost / dataHigh scientific validation cost. Euler/depth estimates from regional, upward-continued gravity may be non-identifiable; test against GP-1012 and synthetic contacts before holdout use.

What is actually ruled out

Only the tested H61-G variants are evaluated: pure basement-step ribbons did not beat random lines under that run's evaluator, while the corridor × slope-ridge locator did. These results do not falsify other buried-fault hypotheses. Earlier H61-B…H61-E ideas remain unimplemented; this ranking does not claim they are dominated. Intrabasement density contrasts remain unresolved and are an explicit interpretation risk.
