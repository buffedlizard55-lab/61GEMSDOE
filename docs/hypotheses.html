11 vg + 18 hg => Euler depth		Normal fault has asymmetric throw: hanging wall deeper, footwall shallower, with sharp step and broad rollover. Intrabasement lithologic contact or volcanic edge is symmetric or has opposite polarity. Asymmetry measured as depth difference across HGM maxima along gradient direction, normalized by throw.Asymmetric basin step under fill indicates growth fault, not depositional ramp; catalogue misses because surface expression is subtle or buried.Previous sessions measured throw magnitude but not asymmetry or polarity. This adds directional sampling across step, not just magnitude.Low-moderate: could improve precision 1.2-1.5x by rejecting symmetric ramps, but recall small because many buried faults have low throw (<80m) below gravity resolution.Low: one gradient computation + two samples per maxima, vectorized.

derived: HGM at each height, Blakely-Simpson maxima, track maxima upward to focus to source
11 vg + 18 hg		Upward continuation attenuates shallow sources faster than deep. Basement step (deep) persists to higher continuation, intrabasement contact (shallow) decays. Tracking HGM maxima across continuation heights focuses to source and separates populations.Shallow intrabasement contacts mimic basement steps in single-height HGM; multi-scale focusing removes them, leaving true basement offsets which are more likely buried faults.All previous HGM used single scale. This implements classic potential-field multi-scale edge detection (Blakely & Simpson 1986 + upward continuation).Low-moderate: could improve precision 1.3x, but requires careful filter tuning and may reduce recall if continuation too high.Low: two extra FFTs at same grid, plus maxima tracking.
