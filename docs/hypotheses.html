own inversion -> horizontal-gradient maxima + measured throw		A basin-margin or intrabasin normal fault puts a step in the basement surface. Gravity of a thin slab is proportional to its thickness, so the step appears as a ridge of the horizontal-gradient magnitude of the depth-to-basement surface. The ridge's throw (depth difference across the ridge) is the fault's displacement.80% of Nevada is covered by Cenozoic deposits and these are thicker than 1 km over about 20% of the state (Blakely & Jachens 1991). A fault under kilometre-scale fill has no surface trace to map; the catalogue is built from surface mapping, so it is blind exactly there. The catalogue reports the fault only if a younger scarp or a mapped unit contact exposes it.The sibling repositories invert nothing: they use band 18 (the supplied horizontal gradient) directly, or proximity/strike statistics of the catalogue. This lane re-derives the depth surface from band 13 with the non-linear Parker series, calibrates the density contrast against GP-1012 rather than trusting the supplied slab product, and then filters candidates by MEASURED THROW -- which is what makes a lineament a fault (DD-ABOUT).rank 1one calibrable inversion (seconds at 400 m) + one EDT at 100 m

USGS Geophysical Investigations Map GP‑1012 (Saltus & Jachens, 1995) is the prescribed depth-scale reference. It is a folded paper map (USGS Store product 23063) and its host is outside this sandbox's egress allowance (github.com, api.github.com, codeload.github.com, registry.npmjs.org, pypi.org, files.pythonhosted.org only). It is named here as the source a future session must obtain; the band-15 substitution used instead is recorded as an irregularity.
