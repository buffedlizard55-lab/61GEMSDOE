Method

The lane

Alluvial cover is one reason a fault catalogue misses faults. Buried range-front and intrabasin faults still step the pre-Tertiary surface, and that step is a density contrast that gravity sees. The lane is therefore:

  1. invert the provided isostatic-gravity anomaly (band 13) for basement relief with the Parker (1973) forward series and the Oldenburg (1974) iteration;
  2. calibrate the depth and density-contrast scale against a basin-depth product (Saltus & Jachens, USGS GP-1012, 1995, is regional 1:2,500,000 and is used only for scale) and against the competition's own band 15 "Depth to basement surface";
  3. take the horizontal gradient magnitude of the recovered relief;
  4. emit the gradient maxima as multi-pixel ribbons, because gravity resolves such a step only to a few hundred metres, and a 1-px line would claim precision the data does not support;
  5. report the recovery of withheld basin-interior segments against a random-line control with the same pixel budget.

Inversion

src/gravity.py. Parameters actually used: {'z0_m': 1200.0, 'drho_kgm3': -420.0, 'cutoff_kc': '0.5/z0', 'n_terms': 4, 'n_iter': 6, 'filter': 'Gaussian band-pass exp(-(k z0 /0.5)^2)'}

Filter design matters and cost a full debugging cycle. With the textbook Oldenburg low-pass L(k) = 1/(1+(k/kc)^(2n)) the deconvolution gain exp(|k| z0) * L(k) peaks at k* = 2n/z0 and then grows, so the iteration overflows — measured as RuntimeWarning: overflow encountered in multiply plus an all-NaN result, after 257 s of compute. A Gaussian band-pass with kc = 0.5/z0 bounds the gain at exp(0.25) = 1.28 and decays afterwards.

Verification: Pearson r = -0.1822, Spearman (approximate, decimated) = -0.1457 against the competition's own band 15 over 5167373 footprint pixels. That correlation is the honest check that the inversion recovers real basin structure. Note the magnitude: r = −0.18 is a weak agreement, which is itself a finding and is recorded under Limitations. The most likely reason is that band 13 is an isostatic residual, whose long-wavelength content that carries most of the basin-depth signal has already been removed.

The metric and why the emission is shaped the way it is

src/gems_metric.py holds a verbatim transcription. Two structural facts drive the design:

  • TP_w takes a maximum over an R = 3 px neighbourhood, so several prediction pixels near one ground-truth pixel buy nothing extra in the numerator, while each adds alpha = 0.2 of its own discounted mass to the denominator. → emit sparse and hard, not soft and everywhere.
  • FP_w is discounted by 1 − k(d), so a prediction within 300 m of a real fault costs almost nothing. → a ribbon is a cheap hedge against location error, which is exactly what "multi-line ribbon" is for.

Holdout protocol

src/holdout.py. Whole fault segments inside contiguous row-blocks are withheld, with a 6 px buffer; catalogue-derived features may only see the remaining visible faults; scoring is pooled DTI against the withheld pixels. The random-line control uses the identical pixel budget. A block bootstrap over 400 spatial blocks gives the 95% interval.
