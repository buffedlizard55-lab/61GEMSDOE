Geophysical method and calibration

  • Parker, R. L. (1973), “The rapid calculation of potential anomalies,” Geophysical Journal of the Royal Astronomical Society 31, 447–455. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-246X.1973.tb02307.x.
  • Oldenburg, D. W. (1974), “The inversion and interpretation of gravity anomalies,” Geophysics 39, 526–536. DOI 10.1190/1.1440444.
  • Blakely, R. J. & Jachens, R. C. (1991), regional gravity/magnetic analysis of Nevada, GSA Bulletin. DOI 10.1130/0016-7606(1991)103<0795:RSOMRI>2.3.CO;2.
  • Saltus, R. W. & Jachens, R. C. (1995), USGS Geophysical Investigations Map GP-1012. USGS publication page; official NOAA/NCEI depth grid; official readme. The complete grid is not locally available; no calibration is claimed.
  • Jachens, R. C. & Moring, B. C. (1990), USGS Open-File Report 90-404. USGS/DOI record.
  • Hildenbrand et al. (1999), USGS Open-File Report 99-49, gravity interpretation and density-depth context. Official USGS PDF. Used only as geologic context, not as a replacement calibration.

Training-label provenance

The official rules cite the INGENIOUS Great Basin Regional Dataset Compilation (Ayling et al., 2022; DOI 10.15121/1881483) as a source for training labels. The local holdout uses the available catalogue as a proxy; it is not the organizer's new-fault test truth. The DrivenData data tab requires login, so the local bridge hashes do not independently authenticate official downloads.
