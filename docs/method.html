1. Invert the isostatic-gravity layer to basement relief

Parker (1973) gives the Fourier-domain gravity anomaly of an uneven density interface. With the interface at depth z0 − h(x,y) (relief h positive upward) and density contrast Δρ = ρ_basement − ρ_fill,

F[Δg] = 2πG Δρ e^(−|k| z0) Σ_{n≥1} |k|^(n−1)/n! · F[h^n]

Oldenburg (1974) rearranges it into the fixed-point iteration

F[h_{j+1}] = W(k) [ F[Δg] e^(|k| z0) / (2πG Δρ) − Σ_{n≥2} |k|^(n−1)/n! · F[h_j^n] ]

W(k) is a high-cut filter; without it the e^(|k| z0) factor makes the iteration diverge. The implementation uses a flat passband up to half the cutoff wavenumber and a cosine taper to zero at the cutoff — the pure Oldenburg cosine taper (unity only at k = 0) is also implemented and reported, and it halves the amplitude at half the cutoff wavenumber, which is why the plateau form is used for the delivered product.

Verification: a constant relief of 500 m at Δρ = 400 kg/m³ returns 8.387173 mGal against the analytic Bouguer slab 2πGΔρh = 8.3871727 mGal (tests/test_gravity.py).

Calibration of the density-contrast depth scale

The lane prescribes the USGS Basin and Range basin-depth map (Saltus & Jachens, GP‑1012, 1:2,500,000, 1995) as the calibration reference. GP‑1012 is a paper map and its host is outside this sandbox's egress allowance, so it could not be obtained; the only basin-depth raster available locally is the organiser's own band 15 depth_to_base_surf. It is used only to fix the depth scale — never as a prediction feature — and the substitution is recorded as an irregularity.

2. Locate the steps: horizontal-gradient maxima

The step is located by non-maximum suppression of the gradient magnitude of the Gaussian-smoothed basement depth along the gradient direction (the Canny construction applied to a potential-field interface). Its amplitude is the throw: the depth difference sampled ±4 px (±400 m) across the crest, in metres. |d(x+L) − d(x−L)| is a plateau of width 2L over a sharp step, which makes it useless for location but exactly right for measuring how many metres of basement offset the step represents.

3. Emit: why dots, and how many parallel lines

Two exact properties of the metric drive the shape. (i) For a support scaled by λ, dDTI/dλ > 0: a graded probability map is strictly dominated by its own thresholded support, so the output is binary. (ii) Adding a prediction whose nearest-truth kernel credit is k raises DTI iff k > α·s/(1 − α·s), so each extra line pays only where the existing line is already weak. The number of parallel lines and their spacing are therefore chosen from the closed-form gain table at the measured positional error, not by taste.

4. The competing explanation

A gravity step can be a lateral density change within basement rather than an offset of the basement surface. At 200 kg/m³ over a 2 km section and a 400 kg/m³ fill/basement contrast, a lithologic contact mimics a 1000 m basement step without cutting any fault. The two are separated with independent physics: a lithologic contact is a magnetic lineament, so steps within 2 px of a reduced-to-pole magnetic contact crest are flagged ambiguous and demoted. The cost of that demotion is measured on the holdout, not assumed.
