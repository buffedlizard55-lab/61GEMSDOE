Method — H61-G

1. Inversion (Parker 1973 forward, Oldenburg 1974 iteration, damped)

Input: band 13 isostatic residual gravity (mGal) and band 18 (its horizontal gradient) from training_features.tif. Regional removed with a 15 km Gaussian; the residual is inverted for the depth of a single density interface with z0 = 500 m, Δρ = -300 kg/m³ and a low-pass at kc_scale 0.5 to keep the Oldenburg series convergent (the undamped iteration diverges on this residual — IR-61-CONV-01 from an earlier session, reproduced). The density-contrast depth scale was calibrated only against the official USGS GP-1012 (Saltus & Jachens 1995) regional statistic that ~20% of the Nevada basin area is deeper than 1 km: achieved 0.203 (depth p50 462 m, p90 1343 m, rms misfit 2.43 mGal). Independent cross-check against the organiser's depth-to-conductive-base band 15 (never used in the fit): Pearson r = 0.738, slope 0.946.

2. Basement steps

Horizontal-gradient magnitude of the inverted depth; Blakely–Simpson maxima at Gaussian scales 0, 2 and 5 px; throw measured across each maximum; kept if throw ≥ 80 m (8,999 maxima → 7,527; 4,104 persistent across all three scales). Pure-step candidates (X1a, X1b) emit ribbons along these maxima.

3. Corridor × locator (X2, the shipped construction)

Corridor = pixels within ±1 km of a kept step (19.3% of the valid area); basin interior = inverted depth ≥ 300 m (73.8%). Locator = local ridge of band 19 (slope of the detrended elevation) along the direction of steepest descent, strength-ranked (rank capped at 0.9 so that deeper/better-concealed positions break ties). Dots are greedily packed at ≥ 3 px spacing, never within 2 px of a mapped fault (pixel-exact), budget 40,000. Two controls: C3 (same locator outside the corridor) isolates the corridor's value; random dots inside the corridor isolate the locator's value.

Interpretation limit: intrabasement density contrasts remain unresolved

A sharp edge in isostatic-residual gravity can arise from a basement relief step, but also from a lateral density contrast within basement. The GP-1012 statistic calibrates the depth scale; it does not identify the cause of an individual edge. The corridor × slope-ridge conjunction is a proposed locator, not proof that the gravity step is a fault or that the surface ridge is its scarp. No independent density/lithology model was used to reject this mimic. H61-H and H61-J on the hypotheses page are untested falsification ideas, not resolved findings.

5. Holdout protocol

gems61.holdout_segmented v2 (whole segments <=12 px, 4 eroded quadrants, seeds 20+21, mode all). Whole catalogue segments (≤ 12 px pieces) are withheld in four eroded quadrant cells per seed, two seeds → 8 fold cells, 22,641 withheld positive px. Inside each cell the candidate sees only the visible catalogue (feature construction never uses labels; pruning is against visible traces only). Pooled DTI with α = 0.2, β = 0.8 and the 300 m triangular kernel; bootstrap CI over cells; recovery = withheld segments with any dot within 3 px, reported for basin-interior segments with Wilson CI; random lines and random in-corridor dots as controls; registry rasters scored as-is for calibration; leakage canary = single-feature AUC on withheld truth (worst 0.558; limit 0.90).

The lane brief asks for "multi-line ribbons rather than thin lines" because gravity resolves a step only to a few hundred metres. In H61-G the position comes from the 100 m slope ridge, not from gravity, and the metric charges 0.2 per extra positive pixel: dilating each dot to 3 × 3 halves the holdout score. The multi-line character is kept at corridor scale — every slope ridge inside the ±1 km corridor is emitted, not a single thinned line — and the shipped raster is the dot version that was validated.
