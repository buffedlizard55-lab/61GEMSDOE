Method

The method, and why each choice was forced

1 · Invert the gravity for depth to basement

Input is band 13, iso_grav_anom, of the pinned training_features.tif. The operator is Parker (1973) forward / Oldenburg (1974) iterative, on a 400 m block-mean grid (the field is band-limited to tens of kilometres; 100 m sampling would add nothing but noise). The gravity band is NaN over 58 % of the rectangle, so the gap is filled by multi-scale normalised convolution — a constant fill puts a step along the whole survey boundary and the downward-continuation factor turns that step into tens of kilometres of fake relief.

2 · The non-linear correction is rejected, on measurement

Oldenburg's iteration does not converge on an isostatic residual. The trace is below: iteration 1 leaves the smallest forward residual; every later correction makes it worse and the relief grows without bound. An isostatic residual carries crustal- and mantle-scale field, and a single-interface Parker series can only absorb that field as relief — so it does. The lane therefore keeps the first iterate (the low-passed Bouguer slab) and reports the rejected correction rather than hiding it.

3 · Calibrate against the published map, not against the supplied band

The two free parameters are exactly the pair Oldenburg identifies as non-unique: the density contrast and the reference level. Both are fitted to USGS GP-1012, whose published scale is 1 : 2,500,000 and whose only transportable quantities are the median depth band and the fraction of the province deeper than 1 km ("about 20 %", Blakely & Jachens 1991). Selected: z₀ = 400.0 m, Δρ = -600.0 kg/m³, kc scale = 0.25, giving 19.9 % deeper than 1 km. The high apparent contrast is expected for an isostatic residual: the topographic and isostatic masses are already removed, so the residual sees a larger effective contrast than a raw Bouguer slab.

4 · Steps, not lineaments

Maxima of the horizontal gradient magnitude of the depth surface, accepted on the Blakely & Simpson (1986) "greater than at least two neighbours" rule. Each accepted maximum then has its throw measured — the depth range across the ridge in a 5 × 5 window — and only the ones above 120 m survive. The DrivenData problem statement's own ledger row DD-ABOUT is explicit that a lineament is not a fault and that a fault is a structure with displacement; this step is where that is enforced rather than asserted.

5 · Emission under the metric, not by eye

Dots are chosen by a greedy allocation whose decision rule is the metric's own marginal test, dT · D > 0.2 · T · (dT + 1 − k), with a minimum separation so no truth cell is paid for twice. The result is binary, because a graded field is strictly dominated by its own thresholded support.
