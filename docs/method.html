Method — H61-MH

  1. Band-pass the isostatic residual. The supplied band 13 (isostatic gravity anomaly) is decimated to the inversion cell, inpainted across the unsurveyed 58 % by multi-scale normalised convolution, and a 15 km Gaussian regional is removed — Blakely & Jachens' (1991) separation of the basement field from the basin field before inversion.
  2. Invert for basement depth. Parker (1973) forward series / Oldenburg (1974) first iterate (the low-passed Bouguer slab), with the density contrast, reference level and filter corner chosen against USGS GP-1012 (Saltus & Jachens 1995, Gravity and basin-depth maps of the Basin and Range Province, Western United States, 1 : 2,500,000) only — median depth band and the ≈ 20 % of Nevada deeper than 1 km. Achieved: p50 458 m, p90 1330 m, >1 km fraction 0.198. Run twice, at 400 m and at 200 m; the two agree at ρ = 0.999. Sign check: corr(depth, gravity) = -0.833.
  3. Find the steps. Blakely–Simpson (1986) automatic maxima of |grad depth|, filtered by a measured throw ≥ 80 m across the 3 × 3 neighbourhood: 12,572 maxima → 5,579 steps.
  4. Build the habitat stack. 28 channels, all rank-normalised to [0, 1] over the footprint and none derived from the position of the mapped catalogue: the 19 supplied bands, plus inverted depth, inverted-depth HGM, step throw, step indicator, corridor indicator, basin indicator, Euler SI = 0 source depth (band 18 as the field, band 11 as its vertical derivative) and the two directional components of the inverted-depth gradient.
  5. Train out-of-fold. L2 logistic regression, positives = the visible mapped faults of the holdout cell, negatives = 4× random footprint pixels. Per-cell coefficients are in evidence/h61m_holdout.json; the mean coefficients are on the holdout page.
  6. Emit. Rank by out-of-fold probability, one dot per 3 × 3 tile, pruned to > 3 px from every visible mapped trace, budget 40,000 dots. Dots, not ribbons: a 3 × 3 ribbon of the same dots scores 0.0184 against 0.0409 on the protocol holdout.
  7. Validate and check uniqueness. gems61.submission_writer writes the TIFF and re-reads it from disk; gems61.validate runs 19 checks; scripts/run_h61mh.py unique compares against 28 registry rasters.
