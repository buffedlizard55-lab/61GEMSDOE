Next steps, ranked by expected gain per unit of work

  1. Bounded-domain inversion. Restrict the Parker-Oldenburg inversion to the largest valid connected region with a cosine taper, instead of filling 58% of the grid by nearest-neighbour. Cost: hours. Expected: the only route to a credible inversion, and the precondition for everything downstream in this lane.
  2. Estimate the optimum emission count instead of sweeping it. With TP_w a max over the kernel and FP_w discounted by distance, the marginal condition for adding one pixel at hit-probability h is h > alpha*TP_w / (D + (1−beta)*TP_w) where D is the current denominator. That turns 'how many dots' from a hyper-parameter into a statistic. Cost: hours.
  3. Sharpen with the vertical gradient. Band 11 is the isostatic gravity vertical gradient and band 5 its slope. Their ratio is a depth-to-source estimator; multiplying the relief field by a depth-indexed weighting is the standard way to separate shallow contacts from deep basement steps, which attacks the competing explanation in Limitations directly.
  4. Bring in the free official data the mirror already carries. Radiometrics and a lidar scarp stack exist in the group's repositories with hash pins (data/aux_bridge/radiometric, external/dem/lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif). Any use must be checked for lane drift first.
  5. Get a first organizer receipt. Nothing in this project can be called a score until the submission page returns one for our bytes. That is one manual upload and it is worth more than any further modelling.
