Why the frame inverts, and why it is degenerate

The inversion. The live scorer masks known USGS/INGENIOUS pixels and scores only expert-labelled faults that are not in the catalogue. The hide-and-recover frame does the opposite: its truth is a withheld subset of the catalogue. So the frame rewards exactly the placement the live scorer deletes. The measured signature is in the table: the three highest-scoring artifacts keep 5.8–11.7% of their dots within 300 m of the catalogue and score 0.19–0.26, while the best-reported artifact keeps only 5.8% there, scores 0.2778 live, and scores 0.00376 on this frame.

The degeneracy. The lane submission on this page is pruned 2 px (200 m) from the catalogue. Its minimum dot-to-catalogue distance is therefore exactly 300 m, which is the kernel radius R. With k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0), every kernel credit is 0, so TP = 0 and the holdout DTI is exactly 0.0 — while a uniform random line field at the same budget scores 0.00336 on the same frame, purely because random lines are allowed to land on the catalogue. A holdout that returns 0 for the best-known emission family is not a weak instrument; it is the wrong instrument.

What this repository uses instead

  • Metric algebra (no fitting): with DTI = TP/(TP + 0.2 FP + 0.8 FN) and α + β = 1, adding a dot raises the denominator by exactly α, so a dot pays iff its kernel credit exceeds α·s. At s = 0.3195 that is 0.0639, i.e. within ~281 m of scored truth.
  • Segment recovery — a geometric statistic that survives the prune: does a withheld segment have any dot within 300 m? Candidate 36.6% vs random 2.6%; basin interior 28.1% vs 0.6%.
  • The cross-lane operating point: 37,654–44,090 px, binary, off-catalogue. Every artifact in the ledger above 0.26 sits in that band.

Recommendation: stop promoting on the same-catalogue holdout. Any number quoted from it must carry the label HOLDOUT-DTI and the anti-correlation caveat.
