One-paragraph result

The lane asked for a Parker/Oldenburg inversion of the supplied isostatic-residual gravity, calibrated only against the USGS GP-1012 basin-depth maps, and for the horizontal-gradient maxima of the inverted basement surface as candidate buried faults. We did that (method): the inversion reproduces GP-1012's >1 km basin fraction (0.203 vs 0.20) and agrees with the organiser's own depth layer (r = 0.738). But on a hide-and-recover holdout that withholds whole fault segments, pure basement-step ribbons score HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0230 — below random lines (0.0314). Gravity resolves a step to kilometres, the metric pays only within 300 m. The one lane-consistent construction that beats random is to use the gravity step as a ±1 km corridor and let the detrended-elevation slope ridge inside the corridor fix the trace: HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0465 (95% CI 0.0421–0.0507) at 40,000 dots, 1.48× random lines, recovering 351/1999 recovered vs 215/1999 by random lines withheld basin-interior segments. That is real but small: the unpruned dotted h19-5 family already reaches 0.1011 on the same evaluator and 0.2600–0.2778 live, so this file is shipped as a valid, unique, negative result, not as a slot candidate. The analysis page explains why 0.2778 is where it is and what would beat it.
