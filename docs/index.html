The one-paragraph version

Lane: buried structures from gravity inversion. Band 13 (iso_grav_anom) is inverted for depth to basement with the Parker (1973) forward series and the Oldenburg (1974) iteration, keeping the first iterate because the non-linear correction is measured to diverge on an isostatic residual. The depth scale is calibrated only against USGS GP-1012 (Saltus & Jachens 1995), which is 1:2,500,000 and is a scale anchor, not a truth mask. Buried range-front and intrabasin faults are then taken as maxima of the horizontal gradient of basement depth that also carry a measured throw ≥ 120 m, and emitted as a hard-core dot field of 38,000 pixels, every one of them at least 3 px (300 m) from the mapped catalogue — because the official problem page says the test truth is “newly identified faults … NOT INCLUDED IN THE EXISTING USGS fault database”.

Three things follow, and they are the whole story:

  1. The truth is disjoint from the visible catalogue (official page, verbatim). The only raster with dots on the catalogue is the only catastrophic score, 0.0512.
  2. The winners are a thinning ladder on one base. 44,090 dots → 0.2600; 40,199 → 0.2708; 37,660 → 0.2710; 37,654 → 0.2778. Over-emission (61,328) falls back to 0.2449. The metric's own marginal rule agrees: a non-redundant dot pays iff k > α·DTI = 0.2 × 0.28 = 0.056, i.e. within 2.83 px of truth — so the optimum is a budget, not “more dots”.
  3. Basin habitat separates the well-pruned fields. Among fields that are all pruned and all ≈37–40 k dots, live score is ordered by the median basement depth under the dots: 243–248 m for 0.2449–0.2778, versus 148–150 m for 0.0512 and 0.2376. The eligible area's own median is 316 m, so every published field under-weights deep basin fill. That is this lane's premise, and it is the only lever the registry evidence actually supports.

Can we beat 0.2778? The lever is real but this file does not pull it successfully: pushing the habitat ramp to exponent 1.0 moved the median dot to 717 m of inverted depth, outside anything published, and the surrogate DTI fell below every registry raster. The exponent was pulled back to 0.25 and the result is reported as measured, not tuned away. Beating 0.2778 needs a validated instrument first — see the run card.
