One-paragraph result — updated H61-L (this session, arena/a7790b7f)

The lane asked for a Parker/Oldenburg inversion of the supplied isostatic-residual gravity, calibrated only against the USGS GP-1012 basin-depth maps, and for the horizontal-gradient maxima of the inverted basement surface as candidate buried faults. We did that (method): the inversion reproduces GP-1012's >1 km basin fraction (0.203 vs 0.20) and agrees with the organiser's own depth layer (r = 0.738). But on a hide-and-recover holdout that withholds whole fault segments, pure basement-step ribbons score HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0230 — below random lines (0.0314). Gravity resolves a step to kilometres, the metric pays only within 300 m. The one lane-consistent construction that beats random is to use the gravity step as a ±1 km corridor and let a 100 m surface scarp indicator inside the corridor fix the trace. In H61-G that indicator was the detrended-elevation slope ridge (band 19): HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0465 (95% CI 0.0421–0.0507) at 40,000 dots, 1.48× random lines, recovering 351/1999 withheld basin-interior segments vs 215/1999 by random lines. In this session (H61-L) we tested the same corridor with the 1 m 3DEP lidar relief (and seven other lidar bands) aggregated to 100 m: the best lidar variant — relief maxima inside the corridor (any depth, lidar valid where 3DEP covers 76% of the footprint) — scores HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0443 (CI 0.0414–0.0478), statistically tied with the slope-ridge version and likewise about half the dotted h19-5 base (0.1011, ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED 0.2600 live). The union of slope and lidar inside the corridor reaches 0.0482 but is 85% overlapping with H61-G (duplicate gate). All are valid, unique, negative results, not slot candidates. The analysis page explains why 0.2778 is where it is and what would beat it (higher-recall surface scarps, not a stronger gravity prior).
