How it was made (short version)

  1. Invert the supplied isostatic residual gravity (band 13) for depth to basement with the Parker–Oldenburg operator, at 400 m, using the first iterate — the low-passed Bouguer slab — because the non-linear correction is measured to diverge here (forward residual 26.31 mGal after one iteration, 1.40 mGal at iteration 6).
  2. Calibrate the reference level and the density contrast against USGS GP-1012 (Saltus & Jachens 1995) — the published map on which basin fill is thicker than 1 km over about 20 % of the province. Selected: z0 = 400.0 m, Δρ = -600.0 kg/m³, giving 19.9 % deeper than 1 km.
  3. Take the automatic maxima of the horizontal gradient of that surface (Blakely & Simpson 1986) and keep only the ones with measured throw ≥ 120 m across the ridge — a lineament is not a fault; a fault has displacement.
  4. Fuse that evidence with the offset band around the mapped traces and the supplied gravity gradient, and emit dots where the competition metric's own marginal test says a dot pays.

Cross-check: the inverted surface correlates r = 0.526 (slope 0.804) with the competition's own depth_to_base_surf band — two independently derived depth surfaces agreeing on the broad pattern, not a copy of one another.
