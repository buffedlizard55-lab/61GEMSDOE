Why catalogue pruning is a plausible mechanism—not proof of the score

The competition's DTI penalises false positives with α=0.2 and misses with β=0.8; soft true-positive credit falls linearly to zero at 300 m. Thus a broad locator can benefit from sparse placement, while every extra dot has a denominator cost. The measured raster lineage starts with 44,090 dots, removes 3,891 dots within 3 px of the visible catalogue to reach 40,199, then removes another 6,436 such dots to reach 37,654. The last raster is a strict subset of its predecessor, verified by an exact set difference in reports/forensics_current.json (machine-readable audit linked from sources and limitations). Because the competition target is described as unmapped faults, suppressing mapped catalogue traces is a reasonable modeling rule; however, the organizer's private test labels and file-specific score are not available here, so its causal contribution to the displayed 0.2778 is not verified.

There is a second instrument caveat. If validation hides catalogue segments but a candidate was pruned against the full catalogue, the candidate has already removed dots near withheld positives. It can then score artificially low on that particular holdout. The corrected H61-G evaluator withholds whole segments and permits candidate construction/pruning against only the visible traces; this fixes that specific leakage/selection bias, not the missing receipt or hidden-test uncertainty.

How H61-G relates to that account score

H61-G inverts supplied isostatic-residual gravity using a Parker/Oldenburg basement model, calibrating the depth scale against the USGS GP-1012 regional statistic; it tests basement-step ribbons and a step-corridor × slope-ridge locator. Its X2 result is HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0465 (95% CI 0.0421–0.0507), 22,641 withheld positive px versus random lines 0.0314 under gems61.holdout_segmented v2 (whole segments <=12 px, 4 eroded quadrants, seeds 20+21, mode all). It is not directly comparable to an organizer score, nor to numbers from another holdout version. It is also not a submission candidate: the final 40,000-dot raster has 98.5925% raw forward overlap within 3 px of a prior near-blanket raster, exceeding the literal >70% duplicate gate. Its format is valid; its uniqueness is not cleared.
