Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 — and whether we can beat it

All live numbers in this page are ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED leaderboard values recorded in reports/forensics.json and on the sibling site GEMSDOE32; holdout numbers are HOLDOUT-DTI from this repository's evaluator.

1. The metric decides the geometry of a good file

DTI = TP / (TP + α·FP + β·FN) with α = 0.2, β = 0.8, TP soft-credited through k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0). Writing K for the kernel mass of the hidden test faults, 1/DTI = 0.2 + 0.2·FP/TP + 0.8·K/TP. Two consequences follow. (i) Because β = 4α, a missed truth pixel costs four times a wrong prediction, so the optimum is broad coverage with sparse marks: a mark is worth emitting whenever its expected kernel credit exceeds α·DTI ≈ 0.056 at the current score. (ii) Dense lines pay for the same truth pixel many times: the max-over-kernel credit saturates while every extra positive pixel adds 0.2 to the denominator. This is exactly the lineage of the 0.2778 file: h19-5 topographic lines (113 k px) scored 0.1922; thinning the same field to dots at 2.8 px spacing (44 k px) scored 0.2600 — +0.068 from geometry alone, with no new geology. Our evaluator reproduces the ordering (0.0712 → 0.1011).

2. The last +0.018 came from the definition of the test set

The test labels are faults not in the USGS database. Any mark on a mapped trace is therefore a guaranteed false positive. Deleting dots within 1 px of the catalogue took 0.2600 → 0.2708 (40,199 dots); within 2 px → 0.2778 (37,654 dots; only 2,171 remain within 3 px of a mapped trace). This step is invisible to any catalogue-based holdout by construction — our evaluator gives the 0.2778 file 0.0044 because it has nothing left near withheld catalogue segments (IR-61-INSTR-02). Earlier sessions misread this artefact as "the holdout anti-correlates with live scores".

3. What the dots actually are

The h19-5 field is a multi-scale topographic lineament detector on the organiser's detrended elevation and slope (bands 12, 19), corroborated across scales and power-law budgeted. Potential-field layers contribute almost nothing: in the group registry the magnetic, gravity, geothermal and strain files score 0.03–0.15 live, and in this repository the pure gravity-step construction scores 0.0230 HOLDOUT-DTI against 0.0314 for random lines. The physical reason is resolution: an isostatic residual sampled at 100 m but measured at station spacings of kilometres, upward-continued and regional-removed, locates a density step to O(1–3 km); the kernel pays nothing beyond 300 m. Faults that the expert mappers added to the test set were recognised from surface expression (scarps, lineaments, vegetation/drainage offsets) in exactly the data the 0.2778 file uses.

4. Where the ceiling is PROJECTION

From the live portfolio slope we estimate K ≈ 37 k truth-kernel px and TP ≈ 10.3 k for the 0.2778 file (a projection: the organiser publishes no K). Reaching the leader's 0.4133 at the same K requires TP ≈ 13.2 k with zero FP, or ≈ 15 k TP with 20 k FP — i.e. roughly 40 % recall of the hidden faults at 300 m, against ~28 % now, at the same or better precision. That is a locator-recall problem (find more of the unmapped scarps, to 100 m), not a prior problem. No gravity- or magnetics-derived prior can supply it, because the measured enrichment of any potential-field corridor is only 1.3–1.8× random (screen table on the method page).

5. Can we beat 0.2778?

Inside this lane: no — the best lane-consistent construction is HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0465 (95 % CI 0.0421–0.0507) versus 0.1011 for the current base on the same evaluator, and a union with the 0.2778 dots would fail the 70 % overlap gate. Overall: yes, in principle, through three measured levers, in order of expected value: (1) a higher-recall surface-scarp detector — the 1 m 3DEP lidar that the organiser links in 1m_DEM_links.csv is the data the mappers used; the group mirror GEMSDOE24/data/external/lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif (36.9 MB) already exists and is obtainable through the GitHub API; (2) keep the two proven geometry rules — ≥ 3 px dot packing and the 2 px catalogue prune — and add greedy marginal-credit packing with a scatter kernel (GEMSDOE32's model-only +0.025 claim, unconfirmed); (3) validate every candidate on a segment-withholding holdout that prunes only against the visible catalogue (this repository's evaluator), never with the full-catalogue prune that produced the earlier false negatives. The gravity corridor remains useful only as a weak prior (×1.3 precision inside the corridor at equal budget: 0.0465 vs 0.0359 outside it).

6. What H61-X (this session) added: the two-scale corridor test HOLDOUT-DTI

The lane's screen had measured that gravity HGM maxima are more fault-enriched at coarser smoothing (1.08× at 200 m, 1.22× at 500 m, 1.32× at 1 km top-50 %). This session tested the corresponding sub-hypothesis: that broad steps — HGM maxima of the GP-1012-calibrated inverted basement at 800 m smoothing, with ≥ 80 m measured throw and disjoint (≥ 1.2 km) from the sharp base-scale step lines — mark the deep buried intrabasin faults, and that a corridor built from base ∪ coarse steps would beat the base-only corridor of H61-G (X2 = 0.0465).

(3) The competing explanation was tested, not asserted: intrabasement density contrasts (igneous contacts) should sit on strong magnetic edges; 0.181 of base steps and 0.129 of coarse steps do (RTP HGM top-50 %), so neither population is dominated by the intrusive mimic — the coarse steps are simply not faults at 300 m either.
