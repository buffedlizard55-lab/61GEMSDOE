Why 0.2778 scored highest, and whether this lane can beat it

Everything here is measured from published bytes or copied from a receipt. The 0.2778 and its neighbours are owner-reported scores from the group's own sites (IR-61-SCORE-01); the group's own audit file records the base ladder and the prune. The current competition leaderboard maximum (0.3195) is user-reported and is never treated as our own measurement. This session's own numbers are HOLDOUT-DTI on the shared instrument.

1 · What the metric pays for

The score is the distance-weighted Tversky index, α = 0.2 for false positives, β = 0.8 for false negatives, triangular kernel of 300 m (3 px at 100 m). With binary dots, a dot pays for itself only if its truth credit exceeds α·DTI ≈ 0.06 at DTI = 0.28. Two consequences drive everything: (a) graded probability maps are strictly dominated by their own thresholded support, and (b) a dot that lands on the mapped catalogue is a guaranteed false positive, because the scored truth is the set of faults the catalogue does not contain.

2 · What 0.2778 is actually made of

The group's own audit of that file (data/registry/audit-h33-2-b2.json) records: 37,654 emitted positives, 0 on the catalogue, footprint fraction 0.0073, float32, EPSG:32611, 3730 × 3292, the organiser transform, all checks passed, and the description “0.2708 base with every dot at d(catalogue) ≤ 2 px deleted”. The controlled ladder on the same underlying field is 44,090 dots → 0.2600, 40,199 → 0.2708, 38,854 → 0.2750, 37,654 → 0.2778. The gain that made it the group's best file is the deletion of dots near the mapped catalogue, not a better locator. That is the single most transferable fact in this project, and this session applies it (both prune radii are scored).

3 · Why the lane could not reach it before, measured

A gravity step fixes a basement offset to kilometres; the metric pays inside 300 m. Measured this session on the same grid: pixels of the inverted-depth horizontal-gradient maxima are within 3 px of a mapped trace 10.1 % of the time against 8.7 % for a random pixel — a lift of 1.20×. The dip projection moves that to 10.3 %. The best conjunction available — the projected trace intersected with an independent lidar scarp detector — reaches 13.7 %, a lift of 1.57×, which is the first construction in this repository that substantially improves on the bare step field. It is still far short of what the live top files imply.

4 · What would have to be true to beat 0.3195

  1. A locator that generalises across space. Every 100 m locator this repository has tested sits at the random baseline under leave-one-quadrant-out with no training pixel inside the scored quadrant (blocked AUC 0.52–0.62).
  2. Evidence the 100 m grid does not contain. The one source that is free, official and explicitly named by the organisers — 1 m DEM via the competition's own 1m_DEM_links.csv pointing at USGS 3DEP (prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com, project NV_WestCentral_EarthMRI_2020_D20) — resolves scarps of 0.5–5 m that are invisible at 100 m. Obtainability is verified, not assumed: this session fetched the group's public mirrors of the 3 m and 10 m derivatives and the 1 m source URLs and hashes from data/pilot/dem3m/dem_pilot_receipt.json. The 1 m tiles themselves are outside this sandbox's egress allow-list (IR-61-NET-01).
  3. Recall, not precision. With β = 0.8 the optimum sits at higher recall than intuition suggests; the measured budget sweep on this session's field is on the results page.

5 · The honest verdict

Not with the 19 supplied bands alone, and not from this lane's step field even with the dip projection. The projected × scarp conjunction is the best construction this lane has produced and it is still an order of magnitude short. The route with real upside is metre-scale topography inside the gravity corridor (hypothesis #2 on the hypotheses page), whose sources are named and verified above.

The PhD-level answer: why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 and whether we can beat it

Everything below is measured from the published bytes (reports/forensics.json, evidence/registry_habitat.json) or from the group's own reported scores. The group's scores are owner reports, not platform receipts (IR-61-SCORE-01); they are used to order the evidence, never as a promise.

1. What the metric actually pays for

The score is the distance-weighted Tversky index with α = 0.2 (false positives), β = 0.8 (false negatives) and a 300 m triangular kernel, on a hidden truth of faults that are not in the supplied catalogue. Write the emission as a binary dot field. Then a dot that lands within the kernel of a truth pixel and is the best dot for at least one such pixel raises the numerator by k = 1 − d/3 and lowers the false-negative term; a dot that lands on nothing raises the denominator by 0.2. The marginal rule that falls out of the algebra is exact: add a dot iff its expected new truth credit exceeds α · DTI, i.e. about 0.06 at DTI = 0.28. So the game is not "be precise", it is "cover as much hidden truth as possible with dots that mostly land inside 300 m of it".

2. What 0.2778 is made of

Measured on the published file: 37,654 binary dots, zero on the catalogue, median distance 19.6 px from it, 0.73 % of the footprint. It is the exact set difference of the 0.2600 file with every dot within 3 px of the catalogue deleted (verified in reports/forensics.json: top_is_strict_subset = true, 6,436 dots removed). That single controlled deletion moved the score 0.2600 → 0.2778, so dots adjacent to the mapped catalogue were net-negative on the live metric. Two independent readings agree: the group's budget ladder (37,654 → 0.2778; 40,199 → 0.2708; 44,090 → 0.2600; 60,069 → 0.2477; 61,328 → 0.2449) says ~38–40 k dots at ~3 px spacing is the live optimum, and the dotted versions beat their own multiline parents (0.1922 multiline → 0.2600 dotted) because the metric charges 0.2 per extra positive pixel.

3. Why the lane cannot reach it

Gravity fixes a basement step to kilometres; the metric pays within 300 m. Measured here and in the previous session: the horizontal-gradient maxima of the inverted basement surface are enriched only 0.99–1.32× over random pixels inside the 300 m kernel of mapped faults, and the corridor indicator's leakage-canary AUC against withheld faults is 0.510 — no better than a coin flip. So the lane can say where to look and never where to put the dot. That is a property of the data (regional isostatic residuals, 58 % of the rectangle unsurveyed), not of the implementation.

4. Can we beat 0.2778?

Three things would have to be true, in order of how much they are worth:

  • A locator that generalises across space. Every locator we have — logistic habitat, gradient boosting, the single strongest band, the lane's own corridor × slope — scores 0.02–0.05 on the leave-one-quadrant-out test, i.e. at the random baseline. The 0.26–0.28 files must be using a locator that does generalise; from the published bytes its strongest single-band separations are the detrended-elevation slope (AUC 0.66 at its dots vs random) and the magnetic horizontal gradient (0.57), and no single layer separates the top scorers from the rest by more than 0.02 AUC — the signal is in the conjunction, which is exactly what we cannot see.
  • External 100 m evidence. The highest-value untried source is metre-scale topography: USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM over the high-prior tiles (free public domain from USGS The National Map; the competition data tab ships only the three rasters listed in the bridge manifest — IR-61-SRC-01). A 1 m scarp is invisible at 100 m but is the primary evidence a field mapper uses. This session's egress allow-list blocks prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com, so it is named as the specific obtainable source rather than claimed as done.
  • Recall, not precision. Because β = 0.8 ≫ α = 0.2, the optimum sits at higher recall than intuition suggests: the marginal bar is only ~0.06 of new truth credit per dot. Files that emit ~120 k pixels score 0.19, files that emit ~38 k score 0.28 — but that comparison confounds budget with field quality, and the only controlled pair we have (the 0.2600 → 0.2778 set difference) changes the prune, not the budget.

Verdict: not with the supplied 19 bands alone. The honest route to > 0.2778 is a metre-scale topographic locator inside a gravity corridor, which is hypothesis H61-N on the next-hypotheses page and needs the 3DEP tiles named there.
